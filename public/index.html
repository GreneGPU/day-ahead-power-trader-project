01 / How it works

From raw data to a bid. Five steps turn market observations into a forecast, the forecast into a signal, and the signal into a position you can replay.

Selected view

Start with the physical market.

Historical DK1 day-ahead prices meet wind, solar and demand forecasts, weather, gas prices and system-balance history. Prices are the training target; forecasts and lagged observations are the inputs.

prices + fundamentals + timestamps
Selected view

Make every interval comparable.

Align the data in UTC, check coverage, and build calendar and lagged features. At 15-minute resolution, lags of 96, 192 and 672 are one, two and seven days. Each row uses only information available before the decision.

raw observations → aligned feature matrix
Selected view

Transfer knowledge from hours to quarters.

An ensemble trained on years of hourly data gives each quarter-hour an hourly baseline. A residual ensemble learns the 15-minute correction on top. A direct 15-minute model is kept as a benchmark, and every evaluation uses chronological splits.

15-minute forecast = hourly baseline + residual
Selected view

A forecast becomes a rule you can test.

Use the price forecast, its spread to the hourly baseline, or your own formula. Residual demand combines demand, wind and solar forecasts. Thresholds turn the signal into long, short or flat.

signal = demand - wind - solar
Selected view

Size the position. Account for the outcome.

Thresholds request a position; size, costs and loss limits shape the modeled execution. The Prop proxy settles on the next observed day-ahead move and closes any open position at the daily imbalance price. The lab simulates decisions — it never submits exchange bids.

signal → requested position → costs & settlement

01 Define the signal

Upload required: outages in MW. Missing data is never assumed to be zero.

Features you can use

Power forecasts
wind, solar, demand (or load_fc) in MW.
Price forecasts
forecast, baseline in DKK/MWh.
Daily forecast shape
forecast_rank (0 = the delivery day’s cheapest forecast interval, 100 = its priciest), forecast_z (standard deviations from the day’s mean), forecast_spread (day’s max − min, DKK/MWh) and hour (Copenhagen time, 0–23.75). The full next-day curve is known before the day-ahead auction.
Weather history
temperature_lag_*, humidity_lag_*, weather_wind_speed_lag_*.
Gas price history
gas_price_lag_*, plus the daily change and seven-day mean listed below.
System balance history
system_balance_MWh_known_lag96 in MWh.

Replace * with 96, 192 or 672 for 1, 2 or 7 days earlier. Historical features keep their source units and existing lags.

Saved model features (16) · click to insert

Names are exact. Values keep their source units and existing lags; they are not normalized or shifted again.

Formula syntax

Use + − * / and parentheses. Functions: abs(x), sign(x), min(x,y), max(x,y), avg(x,y), clamp(x,low,high). Thresholds use the formula’s output units; inputs are not normalized.

Upload optional data

HourUTC,outages; optionally add demand,wind,solar to replace the saved forecasts. Use forecasts or outage information known before each decision.

Using saved wind, solar, demand and lagged model features. Only outages need an upload.

Columns: HourUTC,Signal. Use timestamps with a timezone, e.g. Z for UTC. Include every interval in your test period.

02 Set the rules

Size is 1× at the entry thresholds and reaches the maximum at the outer thresholds. Step jumps straight to the maximum beyond them; scale ramps up linearly in between. Costs apply to every change in MWh, including resizing. Prop proxy only.

Repeated tuning on these dates is exploratory, not independent validation.
